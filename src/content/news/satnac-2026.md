The Intelligent Connectivity Group attended the 2026 Southern Africa Telecommunication Networks and Applications Conference (SATNAC), held from 11 to 15 October at Champagne Sports Resort in the Central Drakensberg, KwaZulu-Natal. SATNAC is the flagship conference of the Telkom Centre of Excellence programme and this year's theme was *AI as the Last Mile: Building the Sustainable Digital Enterprise*.

Our team contributed full papers across 5G, non-terrestrial networks, programmable data planes and intelligent traffic management. The conference was also an opportunity to discuss early-stage work with researchers and industry colleagues.

## Full-paper highlights

### Smarter treatment of encrypted Teams traffic

**Manare Rammutla** presented *In-Network Classification and QoS Prioritization of Encrypted Microsoft Teams Traffic Using P4 and Machine Learning*. The work shows how a programmable P4 data plane can classify encrypted Microsoft Teams voice, video, signalling and background traffic from packet metadata, then apply quality-of-service treatment without inspecting payloads. The evaluated pipeline substantially reduced voice and video loss under congestion.

### Surgical telementoring over 5G satellite links

**Maurine Chepkoech** presented *Cross-Layer Evaluation of Surgical Telementoring over 3GPP Release-17 Transparent 5G NTN with TLE-Driven LEO Dynamics*, co-authored by **Humphrey Owuor Otieno** and collaborators. The paper uses an OpenAirInterface testbed and orbit-derived satellite conditions to examine whether video, audio and interactive telestration can remain usable for surgical telementoring over 5G non-terrestrial networks.

### A nomadic 5G node for search and rescue

**Innocent Nhlanhla Makhubela** presented *Terrain-Aware Positioning of UAV-Mounted Nomadic 5G Nodes for Search-and-Rescue Robotics*. The study connects a real 5G stack to a terrain-aware robotics simulation and compares a hovering UAV-mounted base station with a continuously roaming one. It shows why the best placement strategy depends strongly on terrain and blockage.

### Reliable video over transparent LEO 5G NTN

**Talifhani Nemaangani** presented *Experimental Performance Characterisation of Monolithic and CU-DU-Disaggregated OpenAirInterface-Based 5G Transparent LEO NTN Deployments*. The research compares two OpenAirInterface base-station architectures over a simulated transparent low-Earth-orbit channel, focusing on the delivery of live video for remote and rural settings.

### Confidence-aware traffic classification for network slicing

**Molefi Makuebu** presented *Confidence-Aware Two-Tier Payload-Independent Traffic Classification for 5G Network Slicing*. The paper proposes an edge-intelligence framework that accepts high-confidence traffic classifications quickly and sends only uncertain cases to a second model. In the reported evaluation, this selective approach improved classification performance while reducing inference latency.

### Multi-tenant 5G infrastructure on commodity hardware

**Tariro Mukute** led *A Nomadic Multi-Tenant 5G Node on Commodity Hardware: Architecture, Isolation Planes and Feasibility*. The work demonstrates two independent 5G operator tenants running on a portable commodity-server node, helping investigate practical infrastructure sharing for research testbeds, community networks and rural deployments.

## Work in progress

**Pareekshit Sachan** and **Victor Chabeli** also presented work-in-progress papers. SATNAC's work-in-progress track provides a space to share developing research, receive feedback and build connections before a project reaches its full-paper stage.
